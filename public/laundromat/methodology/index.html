LAUNDROMAT EXIT CHECK

Methodology

How the readiness check decides

The check is designed to surface preparation issues before an owner enters a sale process. It does not provide an appraisal, offer, financing approval, legal advice or tax advice.

Published rules

  • Financial proof: weak or unverifiable earnings are a preparation blocker. Buyer diligence guidance commonly asks for 2–3 years of tax returns, P&Ls, deposits/collection records and utilities.
  • Lease: under five years of controllable runway is treated as a preparation blocker. Unknown or non-transferable lease status is also a blocker.
  • Occupancy: rent above the low-20s percent of collections is flagged as a value risk, not an automatic sale failure.
  • Equipment: commercial washers commonly have a 10–15 year life. A fleet beyond 15 years or with material deferred maintenance is flagged for replacement risk.
  • Transferability: heavy owner dependence is a value/readiness drag.
  • Trend: a material collection decline is a readiness drag that should be understood before listing.

Result order

  1. Get the missing information first if a critical financial, revenue-verification or lease answer is unknown.
  2. Prepare first if a hard blocker is present or multiple material drags exist.
  3. Ready to explore a sale otherwise.

Why no valuation number?

A laundromat can have the same SDE as another store and still deserve a very different outcome because of lease, equipment, location, capital needs and quality of proof. V1 intentionally diagnoses readiness rather than pretending to deliver a formal appraisal.

Sources used to calibrate V1

Primary/industry guidance reviewed includes SBA 7(a) acquisition guidance and current laundromat buyer/seller diligence guidance covering revenue verification, lease terms, equipment age and transferability. Thresholds are intentionally conservative and can be revised when better transaction evidence is available.
